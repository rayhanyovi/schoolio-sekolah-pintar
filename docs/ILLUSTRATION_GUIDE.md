# SCHOOLIO — MODULAR PAPER SYSTEM

## Illustration Style Baseline

**Version:** 1.0
**Style Direction:** Option B — Modular Paper System
**Applies to:** All AI-generated illustrations across the Schoolio product, landing page, and marketing materials

---

## What This Document Is

This is the master style reference for any illustration created for Schoolio using the **Modular Paper System** direction. Use it before prompting any AI image generator — Gemini, Midjourney, DALL·E, Ideogram, or otherwise. It defines the visual language, not a specific composition. Every future illustration should feel like it belongs to the same family.

---

## Brand Context

Schoolio (Sekolah Pintar) is a modern school operations platform — not a student app, not a children's edtech product. Its users are school administrators, teachers, students (secondary/university level), and parents. The illustration style must reflect:

- Institutional trust
- Operational precision
- Warmth without playfulness
- Modernity without startup clichés

---

## Core Visual Concept

**Metaphor:** The school as a perfectly organized desk.

Every school operation produces a document — a register, a schedule, a report card, a permission slip, a grade sheet. The Modular Paper System illustrates Schoolio by rendering these operational artifacts as beautiful, layered physical cards and forms, arranged from a bird's-eye view.

The system communicates: _"This platform produces real, tangible, trustworthy records."_

---

## Visual Elements

### 1. Cards and Documents

The fundamental unit of every illustration is a **document card** — a physical artifact representing a school record or form.

**Card types and their use:**
| Card Type | Represents | Header Color |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|
| Enrollment register | Student/class setup | Deep green `#074838` |
| Attendance sheet | Daily operations | Operational green `#0e6b53` |
| Lesson plan | Academic workflow | Operational green `#0e6b53` |
| Assignment tracker | Workflow/submissions | Operational green `#0e6b53` |
| Report card / transcript | Records and history | Teal `#2a8b93` |
| Analytics snapshot | Data outputs | Teal `#2a8b93` |
| Parent report | Trust/visibility layer | Gold border `#d7a12b` |
| Permission form | Security/access | Deep green `#074838` with lock icon |

**Card anatomy:**

- Surface: Cream `#fffaf0`
- Header bar: Color-coded per table above (full-width, ~18% of card height)
- Body: Placeholder text rows — typeset line rules, not real copy
- Borders: Ink green `#13231f`, 1–1.5pt
- Corner radius: 4–6px equivalent — real cardstock feel, not bubbly UI
- Size: Primary cards are the largest element in any composition

### 2. Layering System

Cards are layered like a physical desk — never flat and isolated.

- **Primary card:** Largest, frontmost, 0° rotation. The focal subject of the illustration.
- **Secondary cards:** Medium size, layered behind primary, rotated 1–3° off-axis. Supporting context.
- **Tertiary cards:** Smallest, partially visible, rotated 2–4°. Background depth.
- **Maximum stack depth:** 3 layers. A card must always remain at least 30% visible.

### 3. Paper Texture

- All card surfaces carry a **subtle linen grain texture** — fine diagonal grain, digital (not photographic)
- Ground/desk surface: warm paper `#f8f4ea` with the same subtle grain
- Grain is consistent in direction across all cards in one illustration
- Texture should be visible on close inspection but not dominant

### 4. Stamps and Seals

- Used to communicate status: approved, submitted, issued, overdue
- **Gold seal** `#d7a12b`: Verified, issued, approved — circular with checkmark or initials inside
- **Operational green stamp** `#0e6b53`: System-generated status — square or rectangular, e.g., "Distributed", "Received"
- **Red tag** `#c94932`: Overdue or alert — small rectangular flag at card corner, used sparingly (1 per illustration at most)
- Stamp style: Ink stamp aesthetic — slightly imperfect edges acceptable, not perfectly sharp
- **Maximum:** 1 stamp per illustration unless the illustration's subject is specifically about status/records

### 5. Human Figures

- Simple geometric construction: circle head + rectangular torso
- Optional arms as simple rectangles — only when gesture communicates action (writing, pointing)
- No facial features, no expressions, no clothing detail
- Color: Ink green `#13231f` or deep green `#074838` solid fill
- Size: Small relative to cards — figures provide human scale context, not narrative
- **Maximum:** 1 figure per illustration
- **Optional:** Illustrations without figures are also valid — not every composition needs one

### 6. Connectors and Annotations

- For illustrations that need to show data flow or relationships, use **thin leader lines** (0.5–0.75pt, ink green) connecting cards
- Small annotation tags (narrow rectangles) can float beside cards with a value or label
- Arrow direction: clean right-angle bends, no organic curves
- Use sparingly — maximum 2–3 connectors per illustration

## Color System

### Palette

| Role                      | Color             | Hex       |
| ------------------------- | ----------------- | --------- |
| Desk / ground             | Warm paper        | `#f8f4ea` |
| Card surface              | Cream             | `#fffaf0` |
| Text, borders, figures    | Ink green         | `#13231f` |
| Setup / structure headers | Deep green        | `#074838` |
| Operations headers        | Operational green | `#0e6b53` |
| Records / reports headers | Teal              | `#2a8b93` |
| Verified / approved       | Accent gold       | `#d7a12b` |
| Alert / overdue           | Alert red         | `#c94932` |

### Usage Rules

- **Warm paper is always the ground.** Never use white, gray, or dark backgrounds.
- **Cream dominates** — the majority of visible surface area is cream card.
- **Gold is accent-only.** Never use gold as a card fill or large background zone. Gold appears as stamps, seal icons, dots, and thin border lines only.
- **Red is last resort.** Only one red element per illustration. Only when the subject specifically involves alerts or anomalies.
- **Shadows are warm.** Drop shadows under cards use a deeper warm tan — never cool gray.
- **Teal** is reserved for records-stage and parent-facing content. Don't use it for setup or operations illustrations.

## Composition Principles

### View Angle

- **Primary:** Bird's-eye view — top-down flat, or 10–20° slight overhead tilt
- Avoid steep 3D perspective — cards should read as flat objects, not receding boxes
- All cards are seen from above — header bars appear at the top of each card

### Focal Hierarchy

1. **Primary card** — largest, clearest, most centered. Always identifiable as the main subject.
2. **Secondary cards** — smaller, partially behind, provide context.
3. **Figure** (if present) — at the edge of the primary card, small, non-dominant.
4. **Stamps/annotations** — final layer of detail.

### Spacing

- Cards should not be tightly packed — breathing room between the primary card edges and the frame is ~10–15%
- Secondary cards should partially overlap the primary card's back edge — not float independently
- No card should be so occluded that its header bar is invisible

### Balance

- Primary card: center or slightly left-of-center
- Secondary cards: upper-right cluster (most common), or spread naturally around the primary
- Figure: lower-left or left edge of primary card if present

## What Changes Between Illustrations

The style is fixed. What changes per illustration:

| Variable       | What changes                                                                                                 |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Card types     | Depends on the subject (setup vs operations vs records)                                                      |
| Header colors  | Follows the color-coded card system above                                                                    |
| Stamps         | Present only when subject involves status/completion                                                         |
| Figure         | Optional — include when human context adds meaning                                                           |
| Card density   | Sparse (1–2 cards) for conceptual/simple subjects; denser (3–4 cards) for complex workflows                  |
| Rotation angle | Secondary cards rotate more in operational contexts (system in use) and less in records contexts (finalized) |

## Strict Rules

### Always

- Warm paper ground — never white, never dark
- Rounded card corners — never sharp UI rectangles
- Warm-toned shadows — never cool gray
- Paper grain texture — always, on every surface
- Color-coded header bars — every card must have one
- Primary card clearly dominant in size and position

### Never

- Scrapbook, craft, or collage aesthetic
- Handwriting fonts or chalk effects
- Photographic paper textures
- More than 3 cards stacked (depth limit)
- More than 1 human figure
- More than 1 red element
- Cool gray or neutral shadows
- Glossy or reflective surfaces
- Dark backgrounds
- Gradients or glows
- Real software UI screenshots inside cards
- Cartoon characters or playful figurines
- Children as subjects (Schoolio serves secondary schools and above)

## Tone Reference Words

When briefing AI tools, these words describe the desired outcome:

> organized · warm · institutional · precise · trustworthy · editorial · tactile · professional · calm · document-driven · structured · elevated

These words do NOT describe the style:

> playful · colorful · vibrant · sketchy · hand-drawn · futuristic · minimal-tech · glassmorphism · gradient · bubbly · childlike · startup

## Extending the Style

This baseline applies to all illustration contexts including:

- **Landing page sections** — hero, features, workflow, trust, role cards, CTAs
- **Product onboarding** — empty state illustrations, setup guidance visuals
- **Marketing assets** — social posts, presentations, case study headers
- **Documentation** — feature explainer visuals, help center headers
- **Role-specific views** — admin portal, teacher dashboard, parent app

For each new illustration, identify: (1) the subject, (2) which card types represent it, (3) whether the context is setup / operations / records — then apply the color system and composition rules accordingly.
